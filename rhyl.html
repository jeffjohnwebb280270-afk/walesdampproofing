Rhyl, Denbighshire

Guest houses that became
flats, and never got the airflow.

Rhyl's seafront and back streets are full of large Victorian and Edwardian guest houses converted into flats and shared houses. It is the most subdivided stock we work in, it has one of the largest private rented sectors in Wales, and damp complaints here are overwhelmingly ventilation.

The local stock

What Rhyl is built from.

Converted guest houses

Many rooms, little airflow

Buildings designed for seasonal occupancy, subdivided into permanent flats. Internal bathrooms, blocked chimneys, sealed windows and extractors that vent into voids. The result is condensation and mould in predictable places — corners, external wall junctions, behind furniture.

HMOs

Occupancy the building was not designed for

Moisture load rises with the number of people. A building producing four times the water vapour it was built for, with less ventilation than it originally had, will grow mould whatever anyone does about drying washing.

Seafront exposure

Driven rain and salt

Rendered and pebble-dashed elevations facing open sea, taking salt-laden driven rain. Blown render and salt-contaminated plaster are common and both get mistaken for rising damp.

Older terraces

Cement render on stone and brick

The familiar pattern: a wall built to breathe, sealed in cement, with ground levels raised at the back by later work.

Worth knowing

Complaints, councils and the paperwork.

Rhyl has a lot of rented stock and a correspondingly active environmental health function. Local authorities assess hazards under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and damp and mould scores higher where a child, an older person or someone with a respiratory condition lives in the property.

For a landlord, an independent survey that identifies a ventilation defect and specifies the remedy is the difference between a resolved complaint and an enforcement notice. For a contract-holder, it is evidence that the problem is the building rather than the way you live in it.

What you get

The survey itself.

Internal moisture profile

Readings at height intervals across affected walls, so a pattern emerges rather than a single number.

Salt analysis

Hygroscopic salts separate genuine rising damp from a leak or from condensation. It is the test most treatment quotes skip.

External defect inspection

Render, pointing, downpipes, ground levels, slate. The cause is outside far more often than it is in the wall.

Air and surface conditions

Temperature, humidity and surface readings — the evidence that separates a ventilation problem from water ingress.
